01 Design intent

It should taste unmistakably like green bean casserole

Tender-crisp fresh green beans, deeply browned cremini mushrooms, a silky and restrained savory cream sauce, and shattering fried shallots.

  1. FirstGreen beans & roasted mushrooms
  2. SecondIntegrated fried-shallot sweetness
  3. ThirdSavory cream with subtle Swiss nuttiness
  4. LiftAged sherry vinegar, at the last moment

Critical gotchas prevented in this formulation

  1. Kirkland stock sodium calibration Zero added sauce salt

    Using Kirkland Organic Chicken Stock (450 mg sodium per 240 mL) supplies the exact chloride ions (NaCl) needed to round out savory chicken notes and suppress hollow, propionic Swiss aromatics—while keeping cumulative sodium safe. Do not add table salt to the sauce, do not pre-reduce the stock, and thin reheated sauce with water or milk rather than extra stock.

  2. Water-bath cheese dispersion 165–175°F74–79°C max

    Melting Swiss in a narrow jar set in a gentle water bath prevents water evaporation, keeps the immersion blender submerged without cavitation, and avoids overheating above 176°F80°C, where stock proteins and whey (β-lactoglobulin) coagulate into permanent grit that sodium citrate cannot fix.

  3. Two sequential half-batches ~375 g each

    Making the sauce as two identical half-batches in a wide-mouth pint (16 oz) Mason jar guarantees ideal immersion-blender geometry, lets Half-Batch #1 serve as your live validation test, and yields ~750 g total with zero wasted ingredients. (Full-batch weights for a narrow 1-liter beaker are also provided.)

  4. Fast-hydrating 1:4 xanthan-to-oil ratio

    Cutting the carrier oil from 10:1 down to 4:1 allows xanthan gum to hydrate fully within 5 minutes so you never overshoot into a slick or stringy mouthfeel overnight.

    The source heading reads “1:4 Oil-to-Xanthan”; the weights are 1 part xanthan to 4 parts oil (0.75 g : 3.00 g).

  5. Two-batch mushrooms with late butter

    Browning mushrooms in two batches using high-smoke-point shallot oil—and withholding all butter until lowering the heat for the minced onions—prevents burnt milk solids from turning the fond bitter.

  6. 60–90 second bean chill & post-microwave blot

    Pulling cut green beans out of the ice bath as soon as cold (and draining vertically) prevents thermal contraction from drawing water inside hollow seed pods, eliminating watery sauce purge on Thanksgiving Day.

05 Day before · Wednesday

Prep day

Follow this exact sequence to reuse cookware cleanly and overlap passive heating windows.

  1. Start shallots in cold oil → while heating, prep mushrooms, aromatics, beans, and cheeses.
  2. Toast panko in the clean 12-inch skillet using warm shallot oil → wipe skillet clean in 10 seconds.
  3. Blanch & quick-chill green beans while the wiped skillet heats for mushrooms.
  4. Brown mushrooms in 2 batches → finish aromatics with reserved butter & wine deglaze.
  5. Blend Swiss cream emulsion in a gentle water bath.
1

Cold-start crispy shallots & parallel produce prep

  1. Slice the 400 g peeled shallots uniformly 1–1.5 mm thick on a mandoline using a hand guard.
  2. Place sliced shallots and 600–650 g (2¾ to 3 cups) cold neutral oil into a cold 4-quart heavy saucepan or Dutch oven. Stir gently to separate rings. Turn heat to medium-high.
  3. Parallel mise en place window (~12–15 minutes while oil comes to a boil and bubbles steadily):
    • Trim 900 g green beans and cut into 2-inch pieces. Fill a 6- to 8-quart pot with 4 L (17 cups) water and 40 g salt, cover, and set over medium-high heat toward a boil.
    • Clean 600 g cremini mushrooms (wipe dry; trim only tough stem tips). Chop 400 g into irregular ¼- to ½-inch pieces (Batch 1) and slice 200 g into ¼-inch slices (Batch 2).
    • Finely mince 60 g (⅓ cup) yellow onion, 7 g (2 tsp) garlic, and pick 1 tsp fresh thyme leaves.
    • Grate 80 g Swiss on the small holes of a box grater (leave covered at room temp so it warms up for Step 5) and Microplane 20 g Parmesan into a small airtight container (refrigerate Parmesan).
  4. Finish the shallots: Once rapid bubbling begins to slow (~15–18 minutes into cooking), reduce heat to medium-low and stir constantly, sweeping shallots off the pan walls.
  5. The instant the shallots turn pale golden-blond (one shade lighter than your target color—carryover heat darkens them rapidly), pour immediately through a heat-safe strainer set over a heatproof bowl.
  6. Spread shallots in a thin single layer on paper towels and season lightly with fine salt. Let cool completely until brittle (~15 minutes), then divide or store airtight at room temperature.
  7. Food safety rule: Measure out 27 g (~2 Tbsp) of strained shallot oil for Steps 2 and 4; cool the remaining infused oil and refrigerate promptly within 2 hours (use within 3 days or freeze; never store homemade allium oil at room temperature).
2

Skillet-toast the panko clean-pan first pass

Decide now whether you want the Stress-Free No-Broil Finish (recommended when Thanksgiving oven space is tight) or the 45-Second Broiler Finish.

My finish:
  1. In your clean 12-inch skillet over medium-low heat, melt 6 g (1¼ tsp) butter with 3 g (¾ tsp) strained shallot oil.
  2. Add 30 g (½ to ⅔ cup) panko and stir constantly for 3–6 minutes:
    • If using No-Broil Finish: Toast all the way to a rich, even deep golden-brown.
    • If Broiling Tomorrow: Toast only to light pale blonde so the broiler won’t scorch it.
  3. Transfer immediately to a cool plate (hot skillet carryover burns panko in seconds). Cool completely and store airtight at room temperature. Wipe the skillet clean with a dry paper towel.
3

Blanch, quick-chill & vertically drain the green beans

  1. Prepare a large bowl filled generously with ice and cold water next to the stove.
  2. With your 4 L salted water at a vigorous rolling boil, add half (~450 g) of the cut green beans. Boil uncovered, tasting a pod starting at 3½ minutes (expect 4–6 minutes for standard green beans, or 5–8 minutes for thicker Romano-style pole beans).
  3. Pull as soon as tender through the core with a pleasant snap—zero raw squeakiness or fibrous chew, as there is no oven bake tomorrow to soften undercooked beans.
  4. The 90-second chill guardrail: Plunge beans into the ice bath and stir continuously for 60–90 seconds just until cold, then lift out immediately (leaving cut pods submerged longer sucks water into hollow seed chambers).
  5. Return pot to a rolling boil, replenish ice if melted, and repeat with the second half of the beans.
  6. Stand the colander upright in the sink for 3–5 minutes, shaking periodically so trapped water drains out of the cut ends, then spread on clean lint-free towels and blot dry. Refrigerate in a paper-towel-lined container.
4

Two-batch wet-start mushrooms & late-butter aromatics

  1. Batch 1 (chopped): Place the 400 g chopped cremini and 45 g (3 Tbsp) water in the wiped 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat. Cover for 2 minutes to collapse the mushrooms’ air pockets.
  2. Uncover and cook, stirring occasionally, until all free water evaporates and the pan sound shifts from wet bubbling to a crisp sizzle (~4–5 minutes).
  3. Drizzle in 16 g (1 Tbsp + ¼ tsp) shallot oil, toss to coat, spread into an even layer, and let brown undisturbed for 90–120 seconds per turn until deeply mahogany (~5–6 minutes). Transfer mushrooms to a bowl (do not add butter yet).
  4. Batch 2 (sliced): Add the 200 g sliced cremini and 25 g (1 Tbsp + 2 tsp) water to the hot skillet, cover for 90 seconds, uncover until completely dry, add 8 g (1¾ tsp) shallot oil, and brown deeply on both sides. Transfer to the mushroom bowl.
  5. Late-butter aromatics: Immediately lower burner heat to medium-low (let the pan cool off the burner for 20 seconds if very hot). Melt all 20 g (1½ Tbsp) unsalted butter, add the 60 g (⅓ cup) minced yellow onion, and cook 3–4 minutes until soft and golden while scraping any fond cleanly.
  6. Stir in the 7 g (2 tsp) garlic and 1 tsp fresh thyme leaves; cook 45 seconds until fragrant.
  7. Increase heat to medium and deglaze with 50 g (3 Tbsp + 1 tsp) dry white wine (or 25 g Kirkland stock + 25 g water), scraping the skillet bottom completely clean.
  8. Return all browned mushrooms to the pan, season with 1.2 g (¼ tsp fine) salt and ¼ tsp black pepper, and toss for 1–2 minutes until the deglazing liquid has almost completely evaporated with zero pooled liquid. Cool in a shallow container and refrigerate.
5

Water-bath Swiss cream emulsion

Instructions below run Half-Batch #1 in a wide-mouth pint Mason jar (~15 minutes). Once smooth, transfer to a storage container and repeat once for Half-Batch #2 (or double all amounts in a narrow 1-liter beaker).

  1. Mix the 1:4 master slurry: In a tiny cup or espresso demitasse, stir 0.75 g (¼ tsp leveled) xanthan gum into 3.00 g (½ tsp + ⅛ tsp) fresh neutral oil until completely smooth (¾ tsp total slurry). Set aside.
  2. Set up the water bath: Pour ~1.5 inches of water into a small saucepan and bring to a gentle simmer. Place 75.0 g (5 Tbsp) Kirkland stock and 1.80 g (scant ½ tsp) trisodium citrate into a wide-mouth pint Mason jar, set the jar in the water bath, cover loosely (a small saucer works well), and heat to 165–172°F74–78°C. Swirl to dissolve citrate, then turn burner to low so the jar never exceeds 175°F79°C.
  3. Submerged Swiss emulsification: Keep the jar sitting in the warm water bath. Submerge the immersion blender bell all the way to the bottom of the jar, start on low-medium speed, and feed in the room-temperature 40.0 g (½ cup loose) finely grated Swiss in 3–4 small handfuls over 30 seconds. Blend submerged for 30–45 seconds until glossy, keeping temperature between 160–175°F71–79°C.
  4. Texture-only check: Rub a cooled drop between your thumb and forefinger or press against the roof of your mouth (ignore flavor right now—concentrated citrate tastes sharp before dilution). It should feel completely smooth. Only if physical cheese grit remains after 45 seconds of blending at 170°F77°C, dissolve a tiny pinch (0.20 g) of reserve citrate in ½ tsp warm water and blend 20 seconds.
  5. Warm dairy & stock dilution: In a liquid measuring cup (microwave 45–60 seconds) or small pot, warm 100.0 g (⅓ cup + 1 Tbsp + 1 tsp) Kirkland stock, 100.0 g (⅓ cup + 1 Tbsp + 1 tsp) heavy cream, and 50.0 g (3 Tbsp + 1 tsp) whole milk to 160–170°F71–77°C. Pour slowly into the Mason jar and pulse the submerged blender on low for 10–15 seconds without whipping in air foam. Unsalted stock: add the 1.4 g fine sea salt here (Phase 2).
  6. Strain in miso & spices: Hold a fine-mesh tea strainer with its bottom dipped into the warm sauce and press 5.0 g (scant 1 tsp) white miso through with the back of a spoon, discarding any coarse hull fibers left in the mesh. Stir in scant ⅛ tsp black pepper (and 3–4 flecks freshly grated nutmeg if desired).
  7. Dose & hydrate xanthan: Tare the sauce jar on your scale and drip 2.50 g (½ tsp) of the mixed xanthan slurry (= 0.50 g xanthan) into the center. Return jar to the turned-off warm water bath, submerge the blender head, and shear continuously for 45–60 seconds. Let rest undisturbed for a full 5 minutes.
  8. Verify coating & repeat: At 165–175°F74–79°C, dip a spoon or test bean—the sauce should coat velvet-smooth, hold a clean finger track on the back of the spoon, and pour fluidly. (Only if still too runny after the full 5-minute rest, blend in 6–8 drops / 0.25–0.50 g of leftover slurry and wait 4 minutes.) Pour Half-Batch #1 into a shallow container, rinse the Mason jar if needed, and run Half-Batch #2 identically. Cool promptly and refrigerate.
  9. Half-Batch #2: repeat sub-steps 1–8 with the same half-batch amounts, then cool promptly and refrigerate.

6

Zero-oven vessel prewarm & topping prep

  1. Fill your shallow 3-quart metal gratin dish or 12-inch braiser with very hot tap water and set on a trivet so the metal warms gently without tying up the Thanksgiving oven (or overheating under a broiler and splitting the cream sauce on contact).
  2. (Optional broiler path only): Position an oven rack 6 inches below the broiler element and preheat the broiler during Step 8.
  3. Measure out your three fried-shallot portions:
    • 20 g (~⅓ cup): Crush between your fingers into fine flakes (prevents limp, stringy rings in the filling).
    • 65 g (~1⅓ cups): Keep whole and crisp for the surface.
    • 15 g (~⅓ cup): Place in a small serving bowl for the table.
7

Reheat mushroom cream with crushed shallots

  1. In a wide 12-inch sauté pan or Dutch oven, combine all browned mushrooms, 625–650 g (~2½ cups) of the Swiss cream sauce (hold ~100 g / ⅓ to ½ cup in reserve), and the 20 g crushed fried shallots.
  2. Warm over medium-low heat, stirring and scraping the bottom frequently with a silicone spatula, until steaming gently at 170–175°F77–79°C (~8–10 minutes). Do not boil vigorously.
  3. Evaporation guardrail: If steam evaporation thickens the sauce slightly in the wide pan, loosen with 1–2 Tbsp splashes of warm water or whole milk—do not thin with extra salted stock.
8

Reheat & towel-blot the green beans

  1. Place refrigerated green beans in a microwave-safe bowl with a vented cover (work in two halves if crowded).
  2. Microwave on high for 90 seconds, toss, and heat in 45–60 second bursts until an instant-read thermometer reads at least 165°F74°C in multiple spots. (Alternative: steam over boiling water for 3–4 minutes to 165°F74°C.)
  3. Purge blot: Immediately tip the steaming beans onto a clean, dry lint-free kitchen towel for 20–30 seconds and lift the towel corners gently to blot away any hot condensation purged from the pods before they touch the sauce.
9

Fold, verify temperature & calibrate off-heat acid

  1. Fold the hot, blotted green beans gently into the 175°F79°C mushroom cream mixture.
  2. Check coverage: beans should be glossily coated with modest creamy pools between pods, never drowning. Warm and fold in small spoonfuls of your reserved sauce only if needed. Verify the combined center reads at least 165°F74°C (ideally ~170°F77°C).
  3. Remove the pan from the burner. Whisk 5 g (1 tsp) aged sherry vinegar with 1 tsp warm water or milk in a small cup, drizzle broadly over the surface, and fold thoroughly so neat acid never shocks a single hot spot.
  4. Complete-bite calibration: Put one bean, one mushroom, sauce, a pinch of Parmesan-panko, and a crisp shallot on a fork, blow to eating temperature (~135°F57°C), and adjust in this exact order:
    • Still slightly rich or heavy? Drizzle in another ½ to 1 tsp (2.5–5 g) diluted sherry vinegar.
    • Wants deeper savory backbone? Stir in ¼ tsp (1 g) fish sauce, taste, and add at most another ¼ tsp (fish sauce should remain completely invisible).
    • Needs pure salt? Add a tiny pinch of fine sea salt only if neither vinegar nor fish sauce is needed.
10

Dual-crunch assembly & service

  1. Dump the warm water out of your serving dish and wipe it bone-dry. Spoon the piping-hot casserole mixture evenly into the warm dish.
  2. Toss the room-temperature toasted panko with the 20 g wispy Microplane-grated Parmesan and scatter evenly across the top, leaving a narrow border of glossy green beans peeking out at the edges.
  3. Finish by your chosen path:
    • Path A — Zero-oven residual melt (recommended): Because you toasted the panko to deep golden-brown in Step 2 and Microplaned the Parmesan into feathery wisps, the 170°F77°C steam rising off the casserole softens and clings the Parmesan within 30 seconds with zero oven contention or scorching risk.
    • Path B — 45-second broil: Slide the metal dish 6 inches beneath the preheated broiler with the oven door cracked slightly and do not take your eyes off it. Pull at 30–60 seconds the instant the Parmesan melts and pale crumbs turn golden.
  4. Immediately scatter the 65 g (~1⅓ cups) crisp whole fried shallots across the top after any broiling (plus ½ tsp fresh thyme leaves if desired).
  5. Carry uncovered straight to the table alongside the small bowl of extra crisp shallots.

Served. Happy Thanksgiving!

Storage & food safety reminders

  • Infused shallot oil: Refrigerate strained shallot oil within 2 hours at or below 40°F4°C to prevent Clostridium botulinum growth in low-acid allium oil; use within 3 days or freeze.
  • Make-ahead cooling & reheating: Cool cooked mushrooms, blanched beans, and sauce in shallow containers and refrigerate within 2 hours. Reheat all previously refrigerated cooked components to at least 165°F74°C prior to service.
  • Leftovers: Refrigerate within 2 hours and enjoy within 3 days of initial prep. Scrape off softened toppings before microwaving leftovers and top with reserved dry shallots if available.
